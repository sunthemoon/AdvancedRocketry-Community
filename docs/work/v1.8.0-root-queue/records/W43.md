## How the station sky renders today (read before the code)

- **Registration and the switch.** `ClientBootstrap.onRegisterDimensionEffects` registers one
  `PlanetaryDimensionEffects` for `planetary` (Overworld fallback, `ClientConfig::planetSkyOverride`)
  and one for `planetary_space` (End fallback, `ClientConfig::stationSkyOverride`). The station sky
  switch is `sky.stationOverride` in `ClientConfig`. When it is false, `overridesSky()` is false,
  `renderSky` delegates to the vanilla End effects, and `PlanetarySkyClient.visualSelection` returns
  `null`, so the fog overrides in `ClientSkyEvents` stop as well.
- **Draw path.** `PlanetaryDimensionEffects.renderSky` calls `PlanetarySkyClient.render`, which calls
  `PlanetarySkyRenderer.render`. The renderer owns fixed static GPU meshes from `SkyGeometry`.
- **Context.** `StationSkyContextPacket.handle` stores the context in `StationSkyContextCache`. The
  cache is cleared on NONE, on logout (`ClientSkyEvents.logout`, then `PlanetarySkyClient.logout`)
  and on a Level change (`ClientPlayerNetworkEvent.Clone`, then `PlanetarySkyClient.levelChanged`).
  The context applies only in the Space Level.
- **Body.** `SkySelection.resolve` adds an `Orbit` (body, colour from `OrbitalAppearance`, solar
  intensity) only when the context's body is in the client's `CelestialClientCache` snapshot.
- **PLANETARY and SPACE_FALLBACK.** The client does not tell them apart. Both draw the body when the
  snapshot knows it. A SPACE_FALLBACK context without a known body, or no context at all, adds no
  disc, so the Level's own space sky shows.
  - The renderer draws the orbited body with the sun-disc mesh, tinted, at elevation -25 degrees.
  - Its size comes from `SkyMath.orbitedBodyRadiusDegrees(distance)`.
  - It sits inside the rigid ADR-065 attitude from `StationSkyContextCache.phaseDegrees` and
    `SkyMath.stationAttitude`.
- **SINGULARITY before W43.** It rendered exactly like PLANETARY: Cygnus X-1 (visual profile
  `space`) appeared as a neutral blue-grey disc.
- **Resource reload.** `SkyProfileReloadListener.apply` calls `PlanetarySkyClient.resourceReloaded`,
  which releases the GPU meshes on the render thread.
- **ADR-057's controller effect.** `AccretionDiscRenderer` is a block-entity renderer (48 quads,
  192 vertices). The portal swirl comes from `BlackHoleGeneratorBlockEntity.clientTick`. W43 changes
  neither.

### Design

- **Decision (`SingularitySky.resolve`).** The black-hole sky is shown only when all of these hold:
  - the sky switch of the Level's effect is on;
  - the server's context has kind SINGULARITY;
  - its body is the body the client's snapshot resolved for the orbit.

  Anything else returns empty, and the existing path draws exactly what it drew before.
- **Mesh (`SingularitySkyGeometry`).** Drawn from code with per-vertex colours and no texture. The
  default 32 segments give 480 vertices; the bound is 512. The mesh is centred at height 100 with a
  unit disc radius, like the sun disc. It has three parts:
  - an opaque black disc;
  - a thin bright photon rim from radius 1 to 1.12;
  - an accretion ring from radius 1.35 to 2.4, foreshortened to 0.28 so it looks tilted, with a
    fixed streak pattern and a brighter approaching side.

  Depth is off in the sky, so the order of the triangles is the painting order: the far half of the
  ring, the rim, the disc (which covers the far half's middle), then the near half, which crosses in
  front of the disc. The builder accepts even segment counts from 8 to 32 (at most 15 x 32 = 480
  vertices) and refuses any other count before building.
- **Placement.** `PlanetarySkyRenderer` draws the mesh with the same matrix as the tinted body disc:
  the station attitude, the -25 degree elevation and the distance-driven size. The draw is untinted,
  so the disc stays opaque. The mesh is uploaded the first time a singularity is drawn. It is
  released with the other meshes on a resource reload, a disconnect and a renderer failure.
- **Particles.** While a singularity is shown, the camera has a clear view of the sky, and
  `effects.advancedVisuals` is on, the client spawns vanilla PORTAL motes. Each one converges on a
  point 6 to 14 blocks toward the drawn disc. The direction uses the same attitude
  (`SkyMath.orient`).
  - Particle setting All spawns 2 a tick, Decreased 1, Minimal none.
  - `SingularitySky.emit` caps the attempts at 8 between two ticks, and a failed spawn still counts.
  - It tracks at most 128 live motes and skips spawns beyond that.
  - Nothing is moved or attracted, and no server, packet or FE code changes.
- **Clearing.**
  - `SingularitySky.tick` compares `Context.basis()` (kind, body and controls; a heartbeat changes
    only the sample). A different basis, no context, or the switch off removes every tracked mote and
    forgets the context.
  - `levelChanged()`, the logout cleanup and the resource-reload cleanup call `clear()` as well.
  - The drawn mesh needs no clearing, because the renderer decides every frame from the current
    context.

## Choices where ADR-066 section 7.1 is open

- **What the 512-vertex bound covers.** It covers the whole singularity mesh (disc, rim and ring),
  not only the ring. This is the stricter reading.
- **Replace or overlay.** The dark disc takes the place of the tinted body disc, rather than being
  drawn over it. The singularity is the orbited body.
- **"Unknown context".** This means no context, any kind other than SINGULARITY, or a singularity
  body that the client's snapshot does not hold. An unknown kind byte on the wire is already a
  decoding error in the packet, which is unchanged.
- **"Context change".** This uses the existing `Context.basis()`: a change of kind, body or controls.
  So an owner's rate or distance change also removes the motes, and they respawn on the next tick.
- **Particles.** The ADR caps particles but does not require them. W43 adds a small accent and
  treats it as an expensive accent under section 7.2 (`effects.advancedVisuals`). It also follows
  the player's particle setting. The PORTAL particle type is referenced at runtime, as the
  controller's swirl already does; no asset is copied.
- **Which switch.** Both the drawing and the motes follow the sky switch of the Level's own effect
  (`visualSelection`). For the Space Level that is `sky.stationOverride`.
- **Art.** Everything is drawn from code, so no texture and no provenance entry are needed. No
  legacy texture was imported.
- **Animation.** The mesh is static and turns only with the station attitude. A spinning ring would
  need a per-frame upload or a different painting split, and the sky renderer uploads nothing per
  frame.

## What a V1 reviewer should look at

There is no V1 claim. On a real GPU, with a station orbiting Cygnus X-1:

- the disc and ring at distance 4 (30 degree disc, ring to about 54 degrees) and at distance 190
  (about 1.3 degrees), and whether the rim stays visible when small;
- with nonzero rates on each axis, whether the black hole stays where a planet disc would be and
  turns with the stars;
- whether the disc is opaque (stars hidden behind it), the near half of the ring crosses in front,
  and the far half shows only beside and above the disc;
- the colours and brightness against the space sky;
- the motes:
  - faint and gathering toward the disc;
  - absent under cover, with `effects.advancedVisuals=false` and with particles at Minimal;
  - gone at once after warping away or setting `sky.stationOverride=false`, when the vanilla End
    sky returns;
- a resource reload (F3+T), and leaving and rejoining;
- a generating black-hole generator nearby: `AccretionDiscRenderer` and its swirl still show
  together with the sky;
- shader mods, which no unit test covers.

## Known risks

- **The sun.** For a data-pack singularity whose body has solar intensity above 0, the ring can
  reach the sun's position at small distances. The sun and its halo are drawn after the ring, on top
  of it. The packaged Cygnus X-1 has solar intensity 0, so its sun is hidden.
- **Motes in blocks.** The motes spawn only when the camera can see the sky. Their target points can
  still lie inside station blocks below the player, where the blocks hide them.
- **Look.** The colours, sizes and mote density are untested on a real GPU and may need tuning in
  the final campaign.
