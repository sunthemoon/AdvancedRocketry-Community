`LaserTargetGameTests.java:628` was also inspected. Its registration occurs during synchronous
`fixture.run` setup, not during the later tick callback. The initial conservative analysis merged
different calls' Runnable arguments and flagged it incorrectly. The final guard binds callbacks at
each call site and includes a regression case for this distinction. That file is unchanged.

`GameTestTickDriver` registers one `onEachTick` during setup. Its mutable `next` and `due` fields
advance each sequential chain without inserting into vanilla's map. Drivers are passed through
shared rocket helpers or owned by a fixture created during setup. A second pending continuation
throws rather than silently replacing an assertion. Independent timers/tickers already registered
at setup remain legal. Original immediate-ready behavior, relative delays, absolute tick times,
attempt bounds, timeout annotations, cleanup and `GameTestTickPacer` calls are preserved.

The first full GameTest run found that the round-trip fixture's independent setup cleanup timers
at ticks 20 and 50 had been mistakenly moved onto the same driver as its tick-80 flight chain.
The driver's pending-continuation check failed immediately instead of losing a callback.
The follow-up restores those two timers to their original test-start `helper.runAfterDelay` calls;
the flight chain still uses the setup-registered driver. No check was relaxed to fix this failure.

The guard choice is a **static source scan in a unit test**, not a re-entrant scheduler wrapper.
It uses the JDK compiler's attributed trees, lexical callback traversal and a bounded helper-call
graph, with call-site bindings for forwarded Runnable/functional arguments. It covers unchanged
GameTests as well as the fixed tests. Its fixtures exercise every helper registration family,
recursive polling, deep forwarded callbacks, method references, stored and anonymous callbacks,
constructors and sequences; legal setup and driver transitions are checked separately.
The real source tree covers imported annotations; all synthetic regression fixtures use fully
qualified annotations so neither annotation style can bypass root detection.
No ADR decision or exemption is changed or required for this test-only refactor.

## Mapped-bytecode confirmation

The actual command was `& "$env:JAVA_HOME/bin/javap.exe" -c -p -classpath $jar <class>`, for
`net.minecraft.gametest.framework.GameTestHelper` and `GameTestInfo`. `$jar` was
`C:/Users/Administrator/.gradle/caches/forge_gradle/minecraft_user_repo/net/minecraftforge/forge/1.20.1-47.4.10_mapped_official_1.20.1/forge-1.20.1-47.4.10_mapped_official_1.20.1.jar`.

`GameTestInfo.tickInternal` obtains the map iterator at bytecode offsets 40-53, invokes the
Runnable while that iterator is active at 87-96, and removes the entry at 110-111.
`setRunAtTickTime` invokes `Object2LongMap.put` at offset 6.
`GameTestHelper.runAtTickTime` calls that setter at offset 6; `runAfterDelay` adds the current
tick and calls `runAtTickTime` at offset 10. `onEachTick` and `failIfEver` iterate
`LongStream.range(currentTick, timeoutTicks)` and their generated lambdas call `runAtTickTime`.

The TASK's broader claim about `succeedWhen` is not true for this jar: `succeedWhen`,
`succeedOnTickWhen`, `succeedIf`, `failIf` and `startSequence` use `createSequence`, which adds
to the separate copy-on-write sequence collection rather than the map. The guard still prohibits
nested use of these helpers under the worker rule, and also checks sequence continuations.
The complete disassemblies are retained as local logs in `build/w28/GameTestHelper.javap.txt`
and `build/w28/GameTestInfo.javap.txt`.
