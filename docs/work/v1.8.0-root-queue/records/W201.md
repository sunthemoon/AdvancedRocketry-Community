## Cause and design decision

This is a **fixture geometry defect**, not a demonstrated W164/W199 preparation
or ticket regression. Both hosted logs show the first claim reaching READY_SOURCE
and resuming flight before the fixture injects UNEXPECTED_COPY. The first failure
at `7ac2d1bed` also predates W199's merge (`ef191528f`).

The old same-dimension endpoints were only 11 blocks apart. At source x=18 and
target x=29, both ends' two-block census margins fit in chunk x=1. Their census
therefore deduplicates to one slot. `BaySlotProofs.captured` completes D6 directly,
and `BaySourceProof.captured` removes the capture and advances to RETIRE before
calling the observer. The partial-capture observer requires PROVE, so it never
injects its conflict. After the first claim finishes, the fixture's second-claim
stall predicate waits for a turn the first no longer holds, until stage 2 times out.

The relevant shared-head change is the expanded GameTest layout. In particular,
W189 added the three `rocket_feedback` tests in `d6a0b79ee`, merged at `f0aa16a2b`.
Running that one-block-template batch before `bay_held_proof_capture` shifts the
bay fixture by 15 blocks and reproduces the exact hosted failure locally. The
diagnostic-only source records one slot at x=18/x=29 for both claims. This minimal
reproducer establishes the geometry dependency; it is not a claim that W189 alone
determines the placement of the entire hosted suite. `BayAbsenceCensus`,
`BayCensusColumns`, `BaySlotProofs`, `BaySourceProof` and `RocketRetirementWorld`
have identical Git blobs at `c0b0daab8`, the assigned shared head and the tested fix.

The fixture now puts its target exactly 16 blocks east of its source and asserts
multiple census slots before starting either claim. It still waits for both
claims to reach PROVE, the first claim's real partial-capture callback and the
other claim's queue position. In the reproducer, the unchanged source x=18 now
targets x=34, giving two slots and the intended UNEXPECTED_COPY hold. All 19
existing assertions, both 1,200-tick timeouts, the 1,150-tick fixture wait and all
production bounds remain unchanged. Restart, observer and cleanup bodies are
byte-identical. The two new unit tests cover the old single-slot control and
1,536 signed-coordinate/claim alignments. No ADR choice is reopened; this test-only
fix changes no reviewed persistence behavior and needs no production design note.
