mission record while the real scheduler can complete it. The failed run records that completion
inside the REGISTERED DATA resume window. It does not establish a recreated initial mission or
an improper product lookup. This is a diagnosis, not a fix, a harmlessness waiver, or a passing rerun.

the satellite record and the account. Record equality includes status and readiness time, not just
identity, history membership or count. There is no query counter: the assertion's word "queried"
does not mean that it observed a read. The separate satellite/account equality and consumed-operation
assertions on lines 177-185 are later and were not reached for the failing variant.

For `NONE/REGISTERED_REMOVED_DATA`, stage 0 directly launches one deterministic initial DATA mission
with a 200-tick definition, verifies there is one mission, captures its ACTIVE state, flushes it, and
queues a synthetic REGISTERED receipt. Stage 1 waits for the receipt acknowledgement, installs a
runtime bridge with an empty celestial catalog, verifies fresh physical admission would refuse,
and selects the RESUME branch. This is not a server restart or removal of the satellite definition:
`removePhysicalCatalog` retains the satellite catalog and empties the celestial catalog. The actual
Forge-registered manager remains subscribed to tick events.

The observation that fired is the full mission snapshot equality, explained by ACTIVE becoming READY
with a readiness timestamp, rather than evidence of a second mission. The raw failure message alone
does not identify the false conjunct or dump either list. Attribution to the mission-state comparison
is a source-and-log inference: the preceding PRUNE case cancels its missions and drains all due active
missions; this serial, one-test batch then seeds the only remaining ACTIVE mission. The logged
scheduler pass completes one, inspects one, leaves zero scheduled, and prunes zero. Its completion
updates that record in place, so it necessarily differs from the captured ACTIVE record.

## Why this is a fixture failure, and the proposed follow-up

[SatelliteManager](../../../../src/main/java/io/github/sunthemoon/advancedrocketrycommunity/satellite/service/SatelliteManager.java)
lines 97-146 run the real END-phase scheduler every 20 game ticks. Its event subscription is bound
to the original manager in `AdvancedRocketryCommunity` lines 285-286; replacing `SatelliteRuntime.manager`
does not remove that listener. Replacing the cached SavedData therefore isolates contents from other
tests, but does not isolate this registry from that manager or from writer publication at tick START.

[SatelliteMissionRegistry](../../../../src/main/java/io/github/sunthemoon/advancedrocketrycommunity/satellite/mission/SatelliteMissionRegistry.java)
lines 379-395 drain due entries through `putMission(mission.complete(logicalTime))`.
`MissionState.complete` preserves identity, payload and deadlines, changes ACTIVE to READY and records
`readyAtLogicalTime`. It does not create a satellite or a mission. Registry writer publication
(`SatelliteRegistryWriter` lines 287-315) acknowledges captures and advances epoch bookkeeping; it
does not replace current mission contents with an older frozen copy.

[BayRestartRows](../../../../src/main/java/io/github/sunthemoon/advancedrocketrycommunity/rocket/server/BayRestartRows.java)
lines 43-82 build an already-REGISTERED frozen group. `BayTransferRecovery` lines 286-294 dispatch
RESUME to source resume, consumption, source proof and retirement, rather than OUTCOMES registration.
Those source/receipt operations do not look up or recreate an initial mission. The observed claim
follows exactly that branch and reaches READY_SOURCE despite the deliberately absent physical catalog.
A mission legitimately becoming claimable while a bay operation finishes is expected player behaviour,
not evidence of double registration, lost history or extra research.

The fixture keeps one live registry across all 19 cases, uses a short ACTIVE mission, and compares
it after asynchronous source/receipt work without controlling the scheduler. Earlier CLAIM/PRUNE
variants also pass logical times and `now + 1300` as observed times. `MonotonicMissionClock.advance`
retains logical time on rollback but resets the last observation, so mixing those inputs with live
world observations is an additional fixture-time coupling. No exact clock reconstruction is claimed.
This uncontrolled observation window, the logged in-window completion and the unchanged resume code
support TEST rather than PRODUCT. No product known-issue assertion or product fix is proposed.

For v1.9, or a separately authorized later candidate, split the REGISTERED physical-catalog fixture
from synthetic history/clock mutation. For the quiescent row, explicitly complete the seed before
capturing it and keep the full snapshot, satellite/account and exact consumption assertions unchanged.
Add a separate deterministic ACTIVE-to-READY case that drives one due scheduler pass during resume:
check the same sole mission ID, immutable identity/payload/start metadata, exact expected READY transition,
unchanged satellite/account and exactly-once package/receipt effects. Log expected/actual mission IDs,
status, readiness and logical/observed time separately from satellite count on a mismatch. Retain the
cancel/claim/prune/replace rows and verify the oracle against a recreated-mission negative control.
Do not ignore the failure, increase timeouts, widen log expectations, or disable the product scheduler.
