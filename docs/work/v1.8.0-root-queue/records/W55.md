## 2. NEEDS-DESIGN rows

No row needs the owner. Four rows need a design note or a Root decision first.

**W04 N5 (stray UNBOUND reservation after an in-place NBT load).** When an operator places a projector from an
item with `BlockEntityTag`, or a structure template is placed in a live Level, `onLoad` reserves the constructor's
random UNBOUND identity and `load` then replaces the identity. The fix releases that reservation and reserves the
loaded identity, which changes the reservation lifecycle of the persisted force-field cleanup root. A design note
must say how an in-place load is recognised (the block entity is already in a Level and its previous reservation
is an exact, non-orphan, zero-claim UNBOUND entry); what happens when the root is not operational or is full, or
when the loaded identity collides with another projector's entry; and whether the reservation of the loaded
identity happens at once or at the next chunk load. The GameTest fixtures that load NBT into a projector
(`ForceFieldGameTests.Fixture`) leak one entry each into the shared root today and would change with it. Under
AGENTS.md section 13 this is a persistence change: a one-page design note and an independent read-only review
before it merges.

**W13 N1 (an occupant whose entity chunk is unloaded or still loading).** Root first chooses among three options.
(a) Record the loaded-only limitation in ADR-065's W13 note and the player documentation. (b) Require
`areEntitiesLoaded` for every arrival chunk (at most 16, with no loading): a refusal at preparation, and a longer
pre-spawn readiness wait. This closes only the "still loading" case, and it changes the preparation and readiness
contract that W11b2's escrow integration builds on, so it needs W11b2's agreement. (c) An occupancy source that
does not depend on entities, such as a bounded per-station index of parked rocket regions. This closes the
unloaded-occupant case, but it is new persisted or rebuilt state with a schema, bounds and a migration, so it
needs a design note and a review.

**W21c M1 (pause paid research while the reservations cannot bound the root).** The satellite registry would
expose the W21bF state "reservations exceed the 16 MiB bound" (only a legacy root above its section budgets can
reach it), and the research scheduler would pause paid advances with a new status. The new status is a
`ResearchStatus` value that the research menu sends to the client, with English and Chinese text, so the design
must fix its wire form. It must also choose between pausing every paid advance and charging each advance by
measured time against the 32-per-tick budget, and it needs a test on a restored over-reserved root.

**W22 F8 (a new member is shown the outcome from before they joined).** Root first decides whether to accept
this, which is consistent with ADR-065 section 5.3; changing the "while you were away" wording is another way out.
To change it, invitation acceptance (`StationManager.java:149`) would acknowledge the current revision in the
offline member-notice store. That is a second SavedData write at the membership boundary, outside the station
registry's checked write. The design must say what happens when the notice store is refused (R-029 refuses the
whole store on load) or the acknowledgement does not fit, and whether an ownership transfer acknowledges too.

## 3. The two timing tests (GroundSurvey row 17 and W21b F9, row 12)

**What they measured.** Both tests timed CPU-bound work by wall clock with a few cold samples:
`GroundSurveyScaleTest` took the median of five STARTs, and `StationCheckedUpdateScaleTest` the median of three
warp commits and one ordinary save. Wall time on a shared machine also counts the time other processes hold the
CPU. The probes (`W55/probe-*.txt`; the probe classes were not committed) found:

- the test JVM has Gradle's default 512 MiB heap, and one START allocates about 164 MiB (three to eight young GCs,
  22 to 58 ms of pauses);
- without induced load (another worker's GameTest was running), the first, cold START took 505 to 523 ms, and
  later STARTs 298 to 411 ms;
- one warp commit at 4,096 stations allocates about 115 MiB and is CPU-bound: its CPU time is close to its wall
  time, and the fsync of the 347 KB file is a few milliseconds;
- with twelve busy processes on the twelve logical CPUs, START wall time was 484 to 1,780 ms (the old statistic,
  the median of the first five, would have been 630 ms and failed), while its CPU time stayed at 312 to 500 ms
  and its GC pauses at 27 to 58 ms. The warp commit took 380 to 772 ms of wall time and 281 to 344 ms of CPU.

The failed full-build run (565 ms median, 936 ms maximum) fits this: at least three of its five samples were slow,
so it was sustained machine load, not only the cold first sample.

**What changed.** A new test helper, `testsupport/SpikeCost`, measures a call's server-thread cost: the calling
thread's CPU time plus the stop-the-world GC pause time during the call (its own allocation causes those pauses),
after warm-up calls, because docs/17 budgets non-startup spikes. Both tests keep the 500 ms budget and assert the
median cost: START over seven samples after two warm-ups, and each station spike over five samples after two
warm-ups. Wall times are still printed. The station test's final-state checks (gravity, warp energy and orbit) now
follow the run count; none was removed. Quiet runs gave a START median cost of 348.5 ms (wall 365.8 ms) and a warp
commit median cost of 274.6 ms. Under the twelve-process load, the same tests passed with costs of 378.8 ms and
302.3 ms while their wall medians were 568.8 ms and 693.9 ms (`W55/reworked-tests.txt`).

**Limits.** CPU time is not fully load-independent: with SMT, a busy sibling slows the thread, and START's CPU time
rose from about 265 ms to about 375 ms under the twelve-process load. On Windows, thread CPU time has a 15.6 ms
granularity. The helper refuses to run on a JVM that cannot measure thread CPU time; it does not fall back to wall
time. Root may prefer to call the change of measured quantity a design decision; if so, the evidence above is the
input, and the two commits (`22675ee9`, `66e55de6`) can be reverted on their own. Root could also give the test JVM
a larger heap in `build.gradle` (the reference server has 4 GiB), which this task may not edit.

## 8. Choices where the reviews left it open

- **W01 L1:** the reconciliation releases the binding rather than writing a generation-1 binding, so formation
  stays the only place that binds; it acts only on a binding of the same owner at generation 2 or later.
- **W01 L2:** the `optional` wrapper (the review's first option), limited to the arc furnace that the finding names.
- **W04 N4:** the placed-against state is the projector's, not the target's: the field grows out of the projector,
  and both are already charged. A protection mod that reads `getPlacedAgainst()` now sees the projector.
- **W13 N3:** INFO, not WARN, so the GameTest log checker and its manifest are unaffected. At most one line per
  named-pad selection or pre-spawn recheck, and at most 8 (one per candidate) per legacy selection; all are driven
  by a launch or an arrival.
- **Timing tests:** see section 3.

## 10. Known risks

- **W01 L1** changes how a crash-inconsistent pair of saves is reconciled. It writes only the hatch's binding,
  which formation rewrites in the same pass, and it transfers nothing, but it is a recovery path; Root may want a
  read-only review of `a0250ad6`.
- **Timing tests:** CPU time is less load-sensitive than wall time, not immune (section 3). A heavily oversubscribed
  machine with SMT can still push the cost up.
- **W04 N6 tests** join a connected player through the player list, as `ConnectedTestPlayers` does; each test
  records it before the teleport and removes it in a `finally`. The explosion test counts drops through
  `EntityJoinLevelEvent`, because `getEntitiesOfClass` need not see a freshly loaded far chunk's entities in the same
  tick. Any GameTest that counts drops that way in such a chunk can miss them.
- **Unit suite and the last two commits:** the passing whole-suite run is at `6c7a7eed`. The later commits
  `fc9ef5fc` and `f2562bba` change only `ForceFieldPlacementGameTests`, which `compileJava` and GameTest run 3 cover.
