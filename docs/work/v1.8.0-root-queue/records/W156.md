### History and what the floor protects

- `0ec91523e24f7956c53241aa5ad04ce5c2d619bc` introduced
  [SafeCelestialTravel](../../../../src/main/java/io/github/sunthemoon/advancedrocketrycommunity/celestial/service/SafeCelestialTravel.java)
  under [ADR-001](../../../decisions/ADR-001-FIXED-DIMENSIONS.md).
  `FIXED_FEET_POSITION` is `(8, 80, 8)`: developer travel builds a 5-by-5 floor at
  Y=79, clears three blocks above it, then places the player at Y=80. The original
  Moon generator had one bedrock layer and three end-stone layers, not ground at Y=79.
- `96e92d995b9b8007f0076a4a8e951069b219d080` introduced the rocket selector's
  `max(surface, FIXED_FEET_POSITION.getY())` under the Earth-Moon transfer design,
  [ADR-006](../../../decisions/ADR-006-ROCKET-CROSS-DIMENSION-TRANSFER.md), introduced
  in `a25fac92a0a929f4eb4d53d40b0ace019a4b56c7`. ADR-006 specifies server-owned,
  bounded pads but does not itself mandate Y=80. The selector does not call the
  developer platform builder.
- `d120d4152689022473512329a44cd29a29317b2f` added the accepted
  [ADR-033](../../../decisions/ADR-033-PLANETARY-WORLDS-AND-SURFACE-ADMISSION.md).
  Its physical-admission section, lines 75-81, expressly retains Moon minimum
  landing Y=80, exempts it from the new ground-support requirement, and preserves
  captured recovery identities.
- [ADR-063 section 5](../../../decisions/ADR-063-MATERIALS-ORES-AND-PLANETARY-SURFACES.md),
  lines 255-263 and 314-325, explicitly preserves that behavior over the new terrain,
  acknowledges the unsupported rocket and fall, and requires a separate landing-rule
  ADR to move Moon arrivals onto the heightmap while keeping existing pads and
  recovery identities working. Section 9 also requires Y=80-or-higher arrival tests.

Thus the documented rationale is fixed-pad/developer-platform and existing-arrival
compatibility, not proof of ground support. The developer command actually creates
support; rocket selection does not. Nor is Y=80 a safe void guard: the selector
starts at `minBuildHeight + 1`, raises Moon arrivals to 80, and exempts Moon from
`supported()`. Removing just the Moon clamp would still accept an all-empty footprint
above the build minimum. Empty-column handling needs an explicit rule in the revised
landing policy; raising the rocket into unsupported air is not a solution.

### Choices where the ADR or plan left room

1. **One batch, taken in turns.** All tests of a GameTest batch start in the same tick, and every ADR-075 case uses the
   same fixed pads. The plan allows at most one new batch, so the cases wait for a turn (`MoonPadArea.turn`) instead of
   racing; each holds it until it passes or fails. A failing case releases the turn through its listener, so later
   cases still run. Every case has a 9,000-tick timeout because it may wait for all the others.
2. **Case 7, "no damage".** Creative players are exempt from fall and suffocation damage, so the passenger is a
   connected survival player. A mock player's movement is not simulated on the server, so the decisive checks are
   geometric: after `stopRiding` the passenger stands at the rocket's base height, the block below its feet is sturdy
   and fluid-free, its box collides with no block, its fall distance is 0, and no fall, in-wall or fly-into-wall
   damage was attempted (a `LivingAttackEvent` listener). An unsuited survival player on the Moon can still take the
   environment's vacuum damage; that is not a landing effect and is not asserted on.
3. **Cases 8 and 9, the recorded y 80 destination.** The journal never rebinds a transfer ID to another checksum, so
   the fixture removes the PREPARED record and puts the record built with `RocketTransferRecord.create` in the same
   tick, before any service tick.
4. **Case 9, recovery's report.** Recovery rebuilds the missing destination, logs `REBUILD_DESTINATION`, and may answer
   RETRY_LATER until the rebuilt identity is acknowledged; the next call then reports `KEEP_DESTINATION` for that
   rebuilt entity. The case asserts that no copy existed before recovery and accepts either final report, then asserts
   one rocket at the recorded origin. It persists entity chunks first, as `RocketTransferRecoveryGameTests` does.
5. **Native upgrade case, PREPARED.** ADR-075 asks for a flight stopped at PREPARED that then lands at its recorded
   y 80 origin. For an ordinary flight, restart recovery of a PREPARED record whose source exists is `KEEP_SOURCE`
   (`RocketTransferRecoveryDecision`, unchanged): the rocket stays at its recorded source, as the v0.6 restart matrix
   requires. The driver therefore asserts that for P (PREPARED: kept at its recorded source origin with its fuel), and
   adds D, stopped at DESTINATION_SPAWNED with its destination recorded at y 80, which lands at that recorded origin
   after the upgrade. Neither path calls the selector. Root may want ADR-075's test text read this way.
6. **Size.** `MoonLandingRuleGameTests` would have been 620 lines; its shared case helper is `MoonLandingCase`
   (253 lines), leaving the test class at 393. The selector is 499 lines.
