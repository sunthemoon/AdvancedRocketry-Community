## Changes and design choices

The reproduction confirms finding 6-1: an actual connected survival fixture with flight disabled spends at least 100 zero-gravity connection ticks outside every station region, returns to the Overworld without a loaded station arrival, and arrives with native counter 80 on the unchanged production code. Its next connection tick, before teleport acknowledgment, disconnects it for floating. The test fails with `arrival counter=80` and the native kick log. The accepted reset fixes that exact test.

`SpaceSafetyReturnService.move` resets `aboveGroundTickCount` once, only after confirming arrival, before the announcement. Both station and Overworld returns use it. Additional assertions cover station success, protected refusal and a dropped job; refusals and dropped jobs retain their counter. `GravityFlightBudget`, its allowance values, station thresholds and `SafeCelestialTravel` are unchanged.

Only the safety return's Overworld branch invokes the new Forge-side arrival selector. Without an intersecting rocket entity, the existing destination is unchanged. Otherwise it considers at most 81 columns in a radius-four square, chooses minimum squared horizontal distance, and breaks ties by ascending x then z. Each candidate uses the loaded chunk's `MOTION_BLOCKING` height, rejects fluid at the feet or floor, checks the player's actual box for block/rocket collision, and requires the collision iterator's one-cell margin to be loaded. No chunk ticket or loading lookup is added. Player boxes larger than 2 by 4 by 2 blocks retain the existing arrival rather than expanding the probe budget. The existing TELEPORT protection check runs on the selected column. An unavailable alternative retains the original point and adds `rocket_avoidance=no_free_column` to the existing INFO audit; successful avoidance and the no-rocket case are also distinguished there.

The choice remains a small Minecraft adapter rather than a pure model, so its heightmap, fluid, collision, rocket-query and loading behavior are tested together in GameTests; no new unit-test class was needed. The original no-rocket Overworld test and its expected position are unchanged. Three new rocket tests cover a nearest free arrival, the blocked/liquid fallback, and rejection of unloaded, liquid and fence-collision alternatives without loading chunks or adding tickets. The connection regression verifies reset, announcement, the unacknowledged arrival tick, and another 100 connected Overworld ticks. Existing suit-motion tests are included in verification.

No operator guide previously described W285's allowance, so the celestial data guide now explains the finite allowance, its application to Slow Falling, the Moon's approximately 0.15-block-per-tick slow descent and the remaining flight-disabled kick boundary.

## Read-only inspection of adjacent moves

- `endgame/elevator/ElevatorRides.java`, at its arrival commit, reuses the rider and connection, calls `teleportTo`, and resets fall distance but not the floating counter. `ElevatorEndpointBlockEntity.onPlatform` checks position, not `onGround` or the native counter. A normally grounded departure may already have cleared the counter, but the move itself does not prevent carrying a held zero-g counter into a 1-g arrival.
- `rocket/rejoin/RocketRejoinGuards.java`, in `pin`, also reuses the connection and calls `teleportTo` without resetting the counter. `RocketRejoinPlacement` can change the guarded session's Level and position before pinning. A fresh login starts with a fresh connection, not the previous login's counter, but an unsupported zero-g wait can accumulate a held counter on that new connection before an Overworld placement. The guard does not independently exempt vanilla's connection floating check.

Both are code-inspection risks, not additional reproduced failures. Neither subsystem was changed; Root can queue and verify any follow-up separately.
