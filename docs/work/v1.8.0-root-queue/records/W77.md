### F8: retained claim through disassembly (blocked before code)

Existing order: `rocket/server/RocketDisassemblyService.java:136,164` calls
`releaseLandedReservation` before `execute`. In
`rocket/server/RocketTransferService.java:424-446`, that removes the record in RAM,
queues checked removal, and returns false; its acknowledgement clears session
classification. Only a subsequent request reaches restoration. In
`rocket/transaction/RocketDisassemblyTransaction.java:180-244`, restoration writes
the separate transaction journal, removes the entity, and deletes its successful
transaction record. That journal (`rocket/persistence/RocketTransactionSavedData.java`)
is ordinary dirty SavedData, not a checked durable exclusion of transfer recovery.

Required new order would retain the transfer claim through obstruction/rollback,
durably fence the old transfer before restored resources become independently
available, complete restoration, acknowledge transfer removal, and release the
claim once. Failure before restoration must leave the original claim and resources.
A cut during restoration needs the disassembly journal to decide rollback versus
completion. A cut after restoration but before checked transfer removal must find
a durable exclusion, not just a session set. A cut after checked removal must find
no recoverable old transfer; cleanup/release must be idempotent.

The existing formats have no such acknowledged disassembly exclusion. Moving the
call alone, retaining an in-memory set, or inspecting restored blocks after restart
does not establish it: blocks can be consumed before the removal write completes,
and neither session state nor dirty SavedData is durable acknowledgement. Adding a
terminal transfer state/tombstone or a checked disassembly publication protocol
would change the journal/proof/hold contract beyond the small call-order fix. Under
the TASK stop rule this implementation does not invent that authority. Root needs
to assign/approve that boundary with W11b2 before F8 can be closed.

Required but not claimed tests: real-service obstruction and rollback retain the
claim; first successful attempt restores resources and releases once; native stops
on both sides of restoration, removal acknowledgement and release, including a
failed removal writer and two restarts, never recreate a rocket over restored or
already consumed blocks. F8 remains open, including the existing REGION_BUSY UX.

### F9: retry the acknowledged destination

Existing order: `rocket/server/RocketTransferRecoveryService.java:331-361` creates
a fresh destination, rebinds its UUID, calls `journal.put`, waits for acknowledgement,
then calls `addFreshEntity`. Every spawn rejection repeats the UUID and write.

New order: when no destination is present, use the record's existing destination
UUID on the unspawned candidate. Preserve the same record object and phase when
its binding already matches. Only a genuinely new binding (including PREPARED
adoption of an existing destination) is written and acknowledged. All existing bay,
escrow, final-action and acknowledgement guards remain in place before publication,
duplicate removal, passenger movement and release. Failed spawn changes none of
the journal, sequence, identity, phase or source entities.

A cut before acknowledgement still has the old authority and publishes nothing.
A cut after acknowledgement/before spawn, or after any rejected spawn, retries the
same identity from the checked journal after startup baseline acknowledgement.
A cut after successful spawn finds that identity and keeps one destination rather
than generating another. No new persistent field, schema, hold or replay exception
is introduced. Tests will exercise real entity-join rejection, repeated retries,
checked file reload between attempts, later success and idempotent recovery; they
will count checked file replacement as well as journal mutation sequence.
