## Both moves reproduce

Both tests failed on the unchanged code at `069c5edfd`, each on its first assertion after the move: the player arrived in the
Overworld with native counter 80, and vanilla kicked it on the first connection tick before the client acknowledged the
teleport ("kicked for floating too long", then `arrival counter=80` in the GameTest failure).

1. **Elevator ride.** A survival member, with flight disabled, stands on the station terminal's platform cell with its feet
   1.75 blocks above the terminal. This is beyond vanilla's 0.55-block support probe, so the client is floating. The real
   ride request and the real 100-tick countdown follow, at zero gravity. Three facts let the counter be held:
   - A pending ride is a safety-return transaction, so the station drift return does not move the rider.
   - `onPlatform` checks only the position.
   - The rider is not a passenger.

   W285's allowance held the counter at 80 for the last 20 countdown ticks. The ride's own commit then moved the rider to
   the anchor, and the next connection tick kicked it.
2. **Rejoin placement.** A survival player logs in adrift in the Space Level, 220 blocks up. It is named only by an
   admission, so the guard protects it but has no durable entry to place yet. While it waits, nothing interrupts the
   floating count:
   - The safety return skips guarded players.
   - Life support exempts them.
   - The guard's per-tick `pin` uses `setPos` while the player has not moved.

   Vanilla therefore counted the floating ticks, and the allowance held the counter at 80. After five held ticks the test
   submits the durable entry. The guard's real placement then teleported the player to the fixture pad in the Overworld,
   and the next connection tick kicked it.

## Fix and the choices made

- **Elevator.** `ElevatorRides.commit` sets `rider.connection.aboveGroundTickCount = 0` inside the existing `arrived` branch,
  beside the fall-distance reset. A teleport that another mod cancelled keeps the counter, as it keeps the energy.
- **Rejoin.** The static `RocketRejoinGuards.pin` resets the counter right after its `teleportTo`, only when the player is
  then in the target Level within one block of the target. This is W301's confirmation test, and it is placed in `pin`
  as the TASK names it.
  - The unmoved `setPos` branch, which runs on every guarded player tick, never resets. A guarded player hovering in place
    in normal gravity therefore still meets vanilla's kick.
  - `pin` is shared. The reset also follows the post-P2 safe handoff and any re-pin of a guarded player who moved off its
    pinned position, because each of those is a real teleport.
  - A client that keeps drifting off its pin is teleported back, and its counter is reset, each time. This lasts at most
    until the guard's 200-tick deadline, which disconnects the player anyway. It is the conservative reading of "once,
    after the confirmed move": one reset per teleport that actually happened, never a held exemption. If Root prefers to
    reset only on placement and handoff, the reset would move into `RocketRejoinPlacement`.

## Test design

- Both tests drive the mock connection themselves: acknowledge teleports, send an airborne position packet, then
  `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.tick()`, as in `LowGravityFlightGameTests`. The move under test runs in its installed server
  END handler. Each test uses one `onEachTick` with a phase variable, paces ticks with `GameTestTickPacer`, and has an
  `@AfterBatch` clean-up for a failed or timed-out run.
- Each test asserts zero applied gravity before the move, and asserts that the counter really was held at 80: at least 10
  held ticks for the ride, 5 for the rejoin. After the move, the first connection tick runs without acknowledgement, then
  100 acknowledged ticks must keep the player connected in the Overworld. The final assertion is that the arrival counter
  is 0.
- The elevator test reuses `ElevatorGameTests.Fixture`. It clears the cell three blocks above the terminal, which the
  hovering box reaches, and restores it.
- The rejoin test follows `RocketRejoinReadbackGameTests` for the sidecar, the data swap and the restore. Its pad is a stone
  platform, 11 by 11 blocks at y 300, above the test position:
  - The placement's heightmap finds the pad there, and no fluid, spawn column or natural terrain is involved, so the test
    does not depend on spawn terrain (W304's subject).
  - The platform cells are restored with `UPDATE_CLIENTS` only.
  - The first login, unguarded, saves the player in space. The second login is the guarded session.
