## Investigation in progress

The four supplied hosted logs show the failing unload batches lasting approximately 32 seconds, versus
6.5 seconds in the passing run. They contain no unload-queue or save-future diagnostics, so they cannot
alone distinguish background generation from a ready unload callback that never gets scheduled.

The cached Forge 47.4.10 / Minecraft 1.20.1 bytecode was inspected with Java 17 `javap -c -p`.
`ServerChunkCache.tick` purges tickets and updates distance levels before `ChunkMap.tick` calls
`processUnloads`. That method schedules at least 200 dropped holders, even without spare time, but
drains ready unload callbacks only with spare time or a queue exceeding 2,000 entries.
`scheduleUnload` waits for `ChunkHolder.getChunkToSave()` and enqueues the callback; that callback
fires Forge unload, saves the chunk, and unloads its block entities. The pacer pumps chunk-source tasks,
not this separate queue. A fresh far chunk can become saveable after the tick budget is exhausted.
