## Diagnosis and design choice

The four hosted logs have the same 12 preceding batches. The three failed etcher batches last about
32 seconds; the passing batch lasts 6.5 seconds and has a server-clock catch-up warning during that
wait. They contain no queue or save-future observations, so attribution of those specific failures
to queue starvation is an inference, not captured telemetry.

Java 17 `javap -c -p` inspection of cached Forge 47.4.10 / Minecraft 1.20.1 establishes the mechanism:

1. `ServerChunkCache.tick` purges stale tickets, updates distance levels, then calls `ChunkMap.tick`.
2. `ChunkMap.processUnloads` schedules at least 200 dropped holders even when `hasMoreTime` is false.
   Its separate ready-callback queue drains only with spare time or while its size exceeds 2,000.
   A smaller queue has no guaranteed minimum service.
3. `scheduleUnload` waits for the holder's chunk-to-save future, including the freshly generated
   neighbourhood's generation/lighting dependencies, then enqueues its callback. The callback checks
   for a replacement future, posts Forge unload, saves the chunk, and unloads its entities.
4. `GameTestServer.waitUntilNextTick` runs tasks without sleeping. `GameTestTickPacer` gives
   background work wall-clock time and polls chunk-source tasks, but does not poll the separate
   unload queue or reset the server's spare-time clock. A save-ready callback can remain queued
   after generation finishes. More paced ticks do not remove that scheduling dependency.

The fix services only already-enqueued vanilla callbacks, preserving their save/future/lifecycle
logic. FIFO service can include other already-eligible chunks in the same level; it does not touch
other levels or change other tests' pacing. Reflection uses 1.20.1's `ChunkMap.unloadQueue` field
`f_140125_`, following the existing test-only `VanillaChunkSave` approach, without an AT or mixin.
No ADR choice or exemption was made. The new 64-task cap bounds the test pump; no existing limit increased.
