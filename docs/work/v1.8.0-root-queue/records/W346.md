# W346: B6.5's native case at the source-save boundary

Implementer: Claude (headless). Date: 2026-11-10. Task: [TASK-W346](../TASK-W346-B65-NATIVE-SAVE-BOUNDARY.md), Root
decision R415. Design addendum: [W346-DESIGN](W346-DESIGN.md), committed before the code. Branch `codex/v1.8.0-w346`,
not pushed.

## Result

The save-path check found no reason to stop and no production finding, so the control and the native case were built.

- **The bytecode** (W346-DESIGN section 2): `noSave` makes `saveEverything(true, false, false)` skip the Level
  entirely (data, chunks, entity manager, `LevelEvent.Save`), and stops chunk unloads and the incremental chunk save.
  It does not stop the entity manager's unload processing: a chunk that drops below FULL has its entities stored
  even in a `noSave` Level. In this case the destination column stays FULL, because the transfer renews its flight
  ticket on every tick while T' is live and the ticket outlasts the landing by at least 400 ticks. The case kills the
  JVM a few seconds after COMMITTED, the autosave report checks the destination's chunk in the same tick, and the
  stopped-world oracle would fail on any stored destination.
- **Retirement of T'** (section 2.4): nothing in the forward flight removes a COMMITTED record; after the landing it is
  only moved to the settled set. Later removals (fill, relaunch, disassembly, passenger release) prove their slots
  and hold on a save-off Level, keep the rocket in a successor record, or apply only to governed records. So T' cannot
  be retired while the destination exists only in memory in this sequence.
- **The native case passed** on a JAR built from `5ef21e2aa`, with both restarts: the stopped world had no rocket of L
  at either end and the committed T' with consumed snapshots; restart 1 rebuilt the destination from T'
  (REBUILD_DESTINATION, counts 0/0) with the same consumed operation; restart 2 kept it (KEEP_DESTINATION, 0/1).
- **It can fail**: on a scratch JAR with W342's M1 the same case failed at restart 1 (REBUILD_SOURCE; the rocket came
  back as a stationary source on Earth and never landed).

B6.5's native row is reached by this case on a local JAR. It is not candidate evidence until it runs on C2.

## The control

`arce bay release-test save-boundary hold <dimension>`, `autosave <logical>` and `restore <dimension>`, registered
only with the release-test property, permission 2, and `EscrowTestEndpoints.requireEnabled` before any dimension is
resolved or the journal read. `hold` sets `noSave` and refuses when it is already set; `restore` clears it only on a
Level it holds. `autosave` refuses (a WAIT line, no save) unless the journal's record for L is COMMITTED and
acknowledged and its destination Level is held; then, in the same tick, it reports the destination entity's presence
and its chunk's entity loading, calls `server.saveEverything(true, false, false)`, and reports the skipped and saved
Levels. The held Levels live in a map keyed weakly by the server; nothing else reads it.

## Choices where the task was open

- **The hold's place.** The task says "before the transfer spawns its destination". D6 must finish first, because it
  captures the destination end's slots and waits on a save-off Level, so the hold is placed at the existing
  `B16_GUARD` pause and the guard is then released with nothing changed. No new cut was added.
- **Autosave with a precondition.** The autosave subcommand takes L and saves only once the journal has acknowledged
  COMMITTED and the destination Level is held, checking and saving in one tick. Otherwise it logs WAIT and saves
  nothing. A separate wait and save would leave a gap between them.
- **No ticket of its own.** The control does not add a chunk ticket. The destination column's FULL status comes from the
  production flight ticket; the autosave report checks it in the same tick, and the stopped-world oracle catches any
  stored destination.
- **A sibling runner.** The case lives in `scripts/run_v180_bay_save_boundary.py` and reuses the forced-stop runner's
  session, candidate pin and helpers through a small optional parameter of its `run()`, so the 1,600-line runner and its
  pinned W325/W331 tables stay unchanged.
- **The positive disk read.** The oracle requires the destination origin chunk to have a stored entity record, so the
  absence of L is read from a chunk that exists on disk, not from a missing file.
- **AGENTS.md section 3.3.** The control is a release-test fault control like `/save-off` for one Level, not a
  protection that refuses a player's save, so no risk-register entry or ADR note was written; the task excludes the
  risk register. Root decides whether one is wanted.

## Known risks

- The window depends on the flight ticket: if a later change released the destination ticket at COMMITTED, the entity
  manager could store the destination before the kill. The case would then fail on its stopped-world oracle (no false
  pass), and the design would need a ticket of the control's own.
- The kill follows the autosave by two seconds to let the overworld's queued region writes finish. A very slow disk
  could leave S' on disk at the kill; the case then fails on "a rocket of L is on disk", again not a false pass.
- W345 adds families to the same campaign driver; Root resolves that merge (the family count in its tests).
