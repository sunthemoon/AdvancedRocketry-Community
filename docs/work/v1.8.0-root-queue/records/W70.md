## Scope and verification design

The write scope is `ClassicScaleGameTests.java`, an inert package-private counter seam in
`ClassicMachineManager.java`, and this record. No production behavior, recipes, persistence
schemas, state files, ledger, ADR, build scripts or workflow changes are authorized.

The fixture covers all nine `ClassicMachineDefinition` kinds, including the observatory and
astrobody data processor: 16 of each, with eight of each working after an idle sample. The
controllers share the production Level manager. Native chunk-unload events, unavailable chunks
and replacement block-entity instances establish a real disk unload/reload. Per-controller
inputs, outputs, energy, identity and paid progress are checked, not just aggregate totals.
Timing observations are not assertions and do not establish the reference-node performance Gate.

The 36 remote chunks each contain four non-overlapping machines. Initial recipe lookup is allowed
to settle before 100 consecutive idle samples. Only the fixture positions contribute to work
counters, so unrelated surviving GameTest blocks cannot cause a false failure. Formation and
initial lookup must first increment the counters, preventing a vacuous zero-count pass.

The active machines receive at most 40 ticks of energy before releasing all fixture tickets.
This keeps jobs unfinished even if native unload takes longer than the shortest recipe; energy is
not removed or reset. After a witnessed unload, the fixture stays unavailable for 20 ticks. Reload
must preserve the exact checkpoint and each plug's energy before additional energy is supplied.
Both research kinds are included; processor input is a ready survey fixture, while observatories
start through the production intent. Completed resources are checked for another 40 natural ticks.

No upstream code or assets are imported. No ADR decision is changed.
