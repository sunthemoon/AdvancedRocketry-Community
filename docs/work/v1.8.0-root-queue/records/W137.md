## Step 5: why the L2 launch was discarded

The cause is not determined. W134's log shows the L2 warm-up BOUND at 11:01:27.040 and released with `LAUNCH_ENDED`
17 ticks later, with no `ARCE_TRANSFER_PREPARE` and no `ARCE_ESCROW_SUCCESSOR_HOLD`. Every F1 stall before the ARMED
write logs that hold line (`EscrowSuccessorRetry.failed`), so the ARMED write was acknowledged. After it, production
discards a governed launch without any log line at these sites:

1. `EscrowFlightCheckpoint.begin`, in the ARMED completion. The record stays null, and the launch is discarded, when:
   - the ledger is absent, or `governedAvailable(L)` is false (the writer is unavailable, an operation for L is
     unresolved, L's flight write is still locked, or a frontier for L is frozen);
   - `RocketGovernedLaunchBoundary.allows` is false;
   - `EscrowGovernedLaunch.afterArm` throws; the exception is caught and dropped. It throws when the source envelope
     changed, or when `EscrowFlightJournal.noTransfer` is false; that check needs `actionAuthorityAvailable(L)`, so any
     failed journal write or an unacknowledged change of L refuses it. It also throws when the route is not ACTIVE or
     record validation fails.
2. `EscrowGovernedPreparation.begin`: the completion's record is null, or the boundary refuses it again.
3. F2, after `journal.put` of PREPARED. The countdown continuation is discarded when L's journal state changed before
   its acknowledgement. It is also discarded when it returns false: the source is gone or not operational, the boundary
   refuses, there is no dispatch authority, or `EscrowGovernedSourceAction.issue` refuses the permit. PREPARED stays,
   and F3 starts the countdown itself (`EscrowGovernedWarmupRefusalGameTests.Discard`), so this case alone does not
   end the flight.

`EscrowPreF2Cancellation` only cancels an ARMED tuple that has no record; it never resumes one. So in cases 1 and 2
the launch is over, and the old `flight()` waited for a record that could not come.

The inputs that can change under load are the following:

- in the boundary check: the readiness of either end, an overlapping assembly transaction, and the entity scan;
- in `afterArm`: the journal's `failedWrite` flag and L's acknowledgement.

Both ends were held during F1: the source by the test's tickets, and the destination by the bound warm-up. W134's batch
has no WARN or ERROR line, which makes a journal or ledger write failure unlikely. I did not reproduce the original
discard under load; W139 owns that reproduction.

The next occurrence will name its stage. `flight()` now reports "discarded after its acknowledged ARMED write (serial,
outcome)" or "before any ARMED write". The report then gives `governed`, `actionAuthority`, `resourceMutation`,
`retryReady`, `assemblyTransactions`, `sourceReady` and `predictedPadReady`, as observed on the next tick.

A related fragility, which is not the cause: the test predicts its Moon pad with the legacy selector and journal
reservations only. Production's governed selection also reserves pending pre-F2 destinations, landed reservations and
other rockets' warm-up regions. When the two differ, the test's `FLIGHT_FIXTURE` tickets hold a pad that production
did not choose. During F1 the bound warm-up holds the chosen pad. After a case 3 discard, however, the warm-up is
released and only the flight's own tickets hold that pad.

### Proposed production INFO line (not added)

```text
ARCE_GOVERNED_LAUNCH_DISCARDED transfer=<T> logical=<L> stage=<F1_ARMED|F2_COUNTDOWN> record=<absent|PREPARED> reason=<code> detail=<text>
```

The existing discard callback, `RocketTransferService.governedLaunchDiscard`, would log the line once per discarded
governed launch. It takes the stage and the record from `journal.find(request)`. The site that decides the discard
records the reason:

- In the F1 completion:
  - `GOVERNED_UNAVAILABLE`, with `governedWaitReason(L)` as the detail;
  - `BOUNDARY`, with the failing check as the detail. A variant of `RocketGovernedLaunchBoundary.allows` that returns
    its reason would supply one of `BAY`, `NATIVE`, `SOURCE`, `TRANSACTION_OVERLAP`, `ENDS_NOT_LOADED`,
    `FOREIGN_ENTITY`, `CANDIDATE_LIMIT` or `CANDIDATES`;
  - `ARM_REFUSED`, with the `afterArm` exception message as the detail.
- In the F2 countdown: `SOURCE_GONE`, `BOUNDARY`, `NO_AUTHORITY`, or `PERMIT_REFUSED` with the `issue` message as the
  detail.
- With no reason from a site: `JOURNAL_CHANGED_OR_STOPPED`, when the journal schedule discarded the continuation
  without running it.

The line is INFO, appears at most once per request, and changes no behaviour. The F1 stall path already logs
`ARCE_ESCROW_SUCCESSOR_HOLD`. W139's step 1 has the authority to add discard lines; this is the content I propose for
that site.

## Choices where the task was open

- The reproduction uses the existing exclusive terminal test cut (`EscrowRuntime.terminalCutForTests`) at
  `F1_ARM_ACK`, rather than a new production hook. It needs release-test hooks and a scenario name, so ordinary and
  hosted runs are unaffected. It fires during the first launch's F1 and removes itself, before the scenario's own
  terminal lease cut installs at step 7.
- The warm-up precondition decides from tickets, not from waiting. A ticket counts when its type has no timeout and its
  level plus its Chebyshev distance to a margin column is at most 31, the entity-ticking level. Warm-up and flight
  tickets have timeouts, so they never count. The scan reaches nine columns, as far as vanilla's lowest level (START,
  22) reaches. It adds no tick bound: a pad that can still go cold is awaited exactly as before.
- `close()` follows the task's list: tickets, passenger and rockets. It also releases what the scenario holds
  exclusively: the lease cut, the barrier proofs and the reproduction cut. It does not remove journal records or the
  escrow ARMED entry, which production owns.

## Known risks

- After a failure, `close()` removes rockets even in the middle of a flight. For an F1-stage discard, as in W134, the
  escrow ledger keeps the orphan ARMED entry, because its cancellation needs the removed source. A journal record from
  a failure in the middle of a flight also stays. In R3 this left no further log line. Any follow-on WARN would appear
  only in a run that has already failed.
- The precondition treats every never-expiring ticket as a leak, including the PLAYER tickets of a mock player that an
  earlier test left on the Moon. Batches run one after another, so no other test can hold a pad legitimately at that
  point. A ticket below level 22 that reaches farther than nine columns would not be named; none exists in vanilla or
  this mod, and the test would then wait as before.
- If an L2 barrier proof is still active when the scenario succeeds, `close()` now releases it. The release is
  idempotent and only frees a save-monitor watch.
- `ACTIVE` is a static map from scenario to batch. It is safe because each of the three batches has one test and
  batches run one after another.
