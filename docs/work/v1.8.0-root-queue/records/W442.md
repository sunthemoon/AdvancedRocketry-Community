## Plan before native execution

C6 is `c8fa16e16be87554f26c402546733df6555d15e6`, JAR SHA-256
`9f7e9d1ef708e27fa5778ca3c72ea3b3605f889465ae0d0d1054a890eb808dd4`.
The [K08 record](W440.md) defines the parent worlds and placeholder accounts.

The eligible fastest single machine at these unchanged Earth/Moon orbits is
a full ordinary solar array: `floor(2 * 63 * 1 * 1) = 126 FE/t`, unobstructed
sky, no day/weather gate in a station. An ordinary single solar generator
produces 2 FE/t here; a combustion generator produces 40 FE/t while burning
Forge smelting fuel. The black-hole generator uses a singularity profile's
rate times its configured percent (C6 Cygnus X-1: 500 FE/t at 100 percent),
plain fuel and a formed, owned multiblock in that singularity's station; it
cannot produce on either of these unchanged orbits.

The fifth registered physical producer is `microwave_receiver`: while loaded,
it converts up to four distinct linked solar satellites into
`min(10000, floor(sum(power * orbit_solar_intensity * output_percent / 100)))`
FE/t. It has a 100,000 FE buffer, checks chip/link authority every 20 ticks, and
does not bank unloaded output. Every K08 input here has five inherited DATA
satellites and **zero SOLAR satellites**, so a newly placed receiver produces
zero. Creating a legitimate solar satellite and identity chip requires the
ordinary real-player terminal assembly/launch route; the console's launch
setup route is release-test-only. Neither that hook, an invented chip identity
nor a client is permitted. Consequently it is not an available charging route
for these sources under this task's commands-only constraint. This corrects
the initial pre-run note's mistaken exclusion of satellite-to-FE conversion;
the charging choice and computed array times do not change.

The chosen arrays, single solar, combustion and black-hole generators push
only to loaded adjacent receivers.
Solar array output plugs share a 1,000 FE/t quota and its 20,000 FE buffer;
single solar/combustion export at most 1,000 FE/t. Black-hole export is capped
at 20,000 FE/t. A warp core in a committed Space claim is a stateless receiver:
all that station's cores share 200,000 FE/t credit allowance and a 10,000,000 FE
balance cap. Pending credits fold every 200 ticks and on normal stop into
Overworld station SavedData, not the core's NBT.

The chosen bounded bank has 32 full arrays per selected station, each with
ordinary output plugs and an adjacent temporary warp core. At equal machine
count it produces 4,032 FE/t versus combustion's 1,280 or single solar's 64,
without fueling overhead. There is no finite unconstrained minimum because
the task does not cap generator count; this bank accelerates the fastest
eligible machine without attempting to fill the claim or saturate its credit
budget. A single array already fits the two-hour condition (2M: 793.70 seconds
plus at most 10 seconds to fold; 8M: 3,174.65 plus 10). The bank needs 497 ticks
(24.85 seconds) for 2M from zero, or 1,985 ticks (99.25 seconds) for 8M from zero,
plus at most 200 fold ticks, command/formation/startup/stop time. Native polling
has a fixed 600-second charging bound and 30-second post-removal settling bound;
the detached launcher limits each server invocation to 1,800 seconds.

`two` charges both added stations to 2M. `target-removal` charges owner A to
2M. `cost-change` charges owner A to 8M, also supplying the interstellar energy
precondition. The other stations are preserved. Every touched cell is checked
for air before any placement, all banks lie within their own claim at Y=200,
and all temporary blocks/tickets are removed before final readback/save/stop.
Each delivered source then receives a separate fresh-copy restart qualification.
