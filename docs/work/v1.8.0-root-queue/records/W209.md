## Cause and scope decision

The hosted failure was **before PREPARED and before disarming**, not an accepted
flight losing its outcome. In job `123748136740`, the cutoff batch started at
22:11:49.865 UTC. F1 started at 22:11:50.665 and was discarded at 22:11:50.865 with
`F1_CALLBACK / NO_ACKNOWLEDGED_RECORD`; its warm-up was released at age 3. There is
no preparation or arrival for that transfer. `Flight.tick()` remained in step 3,
waiting for `journal.findByLogicalRocket(...)` to become present, until the
unchanged 2,400-tick deadline at 22:13:50.465.

The batch contains only this one test. The other four escrow-monitor batches ran
separately and passed. Their start-to-next-batch times were approximately 15.70 s
(`monitor_membership_before`), 16.30 s (`monitor_governed_cold`), 14.58 s
(`monitor_governed`), and 14.80 s (`monitor_membership_after`); the cutoff batch
took 120.60 s. The preceding `fluid_cargo_flights` batch had already finished.
All other 1,556 GameTests passed. The job's seven overload warnings ended at
21:53:39 UTC, well before this failure; the failing wait was paced at about 20 TPS.

Counting the console's 20-tick progress lines places F1's start before tick
30,000 and its discard immediately after it. Disassembly of the mapped 1.20.1
`MinecraftServer` and `GameTestServer` confirms the 6,000-tick autosave period and
the progress-line cadence. The historical console lacks the detailed barrier
reason, so autosave attribution there is an inference, corroborated by the
following direct reproduction rather than treated as a logged fact.

An external diagnostic prelude lets the server advance normally to tick 5,965
before starting the unchanged cutoff test. It changes neither the server clock
nor the test's timeout, assertions, stores, or outcomes. In three fresh worlds,
F1 started at ticks 5,990, 5,990, and 5,989. Each real periodic autosave superseded
the source capture: production logged `F1_PROOF / SUPERSEDING_SAVE`, followed by
the same discard as the hosted failure. At ticks 6,012, 6,012, and 6,011, the
monitor displayed `TRANSFER_JOURNAL_BLOCKED`; the source remained loaded and
FUELED, PREPARED was absent, and the barrier reported one supersession with zero
IO failures or other refusals. All three tests then timed out. Thus neither an
unloaded arrival nor an unreported accepted outcome explains this reproduction.

This is the same fixture hazard documented for the Moon launch in
[W184](W184.md). `JOURNAL_PENDING` queues F1; it is not the submitted PREPARED
acceptance cutoff described in [W153](W153.md). The product correctly refused
before that cutoff. No production change or new ADR was warranted.

changed. Its shared flight fixture now waits until the next periodic autosave is
more than 60 tick ends away before submitting an edge, including the fresh edge
after cold-destination warm-up. This is the existing three-force-period F1
fixture window, derived from `EscrowBarrierBudget.CADENCE`.

Step 3 still waits for the real PREPARED record. While it is absent, a new
assertion requires F1's logical lock to remain held. The acknowledged callback
releases that lock and publishes PREPARED synchronously on the owner thread;
neither lock nor record therefore means the request ended, not that it needs
another 2,400 ticks. The failure now names that stage and the monitor outcome.
No automatic retry hides a refused request. The deliberate before-PREPARED
membership refusal retains its separate existing assertions.

`@GameTest` annotations, including every 2,400-tick timeout. There is one added
assertion, no new repository test class, and no changed acceptance cutoff,
