### Item 1: the per-rocket lease

`RocketJournalLeases` (new, package-private in `rocket.flight.persistence`) keeps, for each logical rocket, the journal
mutation sequence of its latest change, and publishes an immutable view after every record change, acknowledgement and
lifecycle change. `RocketTransferSavedData.logicalLease(L, T, serial)` returns a lease that reads only that view, so the
ledger writer can check it on its IO thread immediately before replacement. The lease holds while, for L:

- the journal holds the exact record instance of L that was current at acquisition, or still no record of L;
- no change of L is newer than the acquisition or than the latest acknowledgement;
- L's acknowledged guard is unchanged: the acknowledged digest of L's record, or no acknowledged record of L;
- no other record names the operation's T, or (for a serial above zero) its ARMED serial; a schema-3 record without a
  native envelope still counts as naming every serial, as before;
- the journal is operational, not stopping, and has an acknowledgement.

Another rocket's put, phase change or removal, and the first-landing outbox, do not revoke it. A change of L after
acquisition revokes it for good, so an absence lease cannot come back after a record of L appears and is removed.

The world-wide `acknowledgedLease()` stays for the paths R17 does not list: the dispatcher's route cache and the
settlement normalization permit.

Callers, each in its own file:

- `EscrowFlightJournal.noTransfer` (F1, the F1 callback, the pre-F2 cancellation and F8) now acquires through
  `actionAuthorityAvailable(L)` and `logicalGuard(L)` and returns `logicalLease(L, T, serial)`.
- `EscrowFlightJournal.members` gives settlement O3/O4 and the reconciliation view one lease per distinct (rocket, T)
  that the members' guards name.
- F7 (`EscrowGovernedHandoff`): the proof's lease is `logicalLease(L, T, serial)`; its owner-thread checks at proof

  `requireJournal` checks each member rocket's own guard.

### Item 2: batch re-capture

## Choices where R17 or the design left room

1. **The lease is checked where it was checked before.** At acquisition, at capture and immediately before
   replacement. The owner-thread gates of the listed operations that used to require a quiet journal now require L's
   checked state. A failed journal write still refuses: `actionAuthorityAvailable(L)` keeps the `failedWrite` check, so
   only unrelated pending changes stop refusing.
2. **Leases over several members.** Settlement and reconciliation hold one lease per distinct (rocket, T) that their
   members name, and need all of them. Reconciliation C2 shares the view's lease, so C2 is also per rocket.
3. **History bound.** `RocketJournalLeases` remembers 1,024 rockets. Beyond that, the oldest history folds into a floor
   that is treated as a possible change of every rocket. This can only refuse a lease, never grant one, and only a
   lease that outlives changes of more than 1,024 distinct rockets.
4. **Batch re-capture.** It is keyed on the guard's own record of the refusing request. It applies to `GUARD_REFUSED`
   and to the existing `FAILED_BEFORE_REPLACE` that resolves an earlier unknown outcome after a guard refusal. A
   definite IO failure fails every request, as before.
5. **"Before any IO".** R17 item 4 is read as the review describes it: a refusal before any replacement is attempted.
   The guard is still checked inside the committer, after the temporary file is staged and immediately before the move.
   A refusal while an earlier outcome is unknown still resolves through readback as `FAILED_BEFORE_REPLACE`, which the
   forced-stop runner and its test match by log line.
6. **F9.** Of the review's two options, this keeps PREPARED and reports nothing. Checking `canQueueEffect` before the
   put would instead leave an ARMED orphan for the pre-F2 cancellation whenever two other launches are waiting for the
   journal, which is the transient refusal R17 item 3 removes. Residual: the request's replay-guard entry stays ACTIVE
   for the server session (the guard holds 4,096 entries), and the player gets no SUCCESS message; the countdown
   starts through F3.
7. **F8.** A unit test rather than a native gate check, as the review allows; the dedicated server object is real but
   not started.
