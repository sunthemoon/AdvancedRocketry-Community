### W130-R1: the rule

- **Holds checked before any ticket.** Before its ticket step, every BEFORE_FILL attempt checks three holds that no
  ticket can end, without loading anything:
  - both ends' discovery regions are admissible (`RocketDiscoveryColumns`, computed from T's snapshots);
  - no census Level has saving off (`Level.noSave()`, through the new `Port.saveOff`);
  - every census Level's entity storage gives an own-store proof.

  A failing check holds the attempt (`DISCOVERY_BOUNDS`, `SAVE_OFF` or `UNSUPPORTED_ENTITY_STORAGE`) with no ticket.
  Save-off and unsupported storage each WARN once per Level. The storage WARN keeps W130's text. The save-off WARN,
  `ARCE_TRANSFER_FILL_RETIREMENT_SAVE_OFF`, is new, and no GameTest drives it. Such an entry keeps re-checking every
  200 ticks with no ticket and no load, until the hold clears, T changes or the server stops (RAM only, at most 64
  entries).
- **Three ticket steps per demand (`DEMAND_TICKETS`).** A cold old site needs two: the first ticket loads it and the
  attempt waits on `SLOT_UNLOADED`, and the second, at least 200 ticks later, proves. The third absorbs one failed
  proof. When the attempt that used the third step fails or waits, the entry ends at once. T is kept, its tickets are
  released, and an INFO line logs `ARCE_TRANSFER_RETIREMENT_ABANDONED ... reason=DEMAND_SPENT`. Only a new loader
  demand creates a new entry. That entry still waits out the rocket's 200-tick spacing from its last attempt, so a
  demand never resets W98's backoff.
- **No entry keeps itself loaded.** An entry refreshes D's ticket only in its own ticket steps. So its refresh can keep
  D loaded at most until the end of the demand's third step, about 400 ticks after the first, and then it releases its
  tickets. A later demand begins an entry only if D's projection still finds the rocket loaded at the coordinator's
  tick (W130-R4), that is, loaded by something else. A loader that only the entry's tickets kept ticking therefore
  cannot revive it. A stricter rule, "refresh D only while something else keeps it loaded", cannot be observed
  through vanilla's ticket API: at that moment D is always loaded by the entry's previous ticket.
- **Unchanged.** At most four starts per tick, one attempt per rocket per 200 ticks (kept through an abandoned or
  recreated entry), the existing 400-tick ordinary ticket, and nothing at startup. No proof, lease, budget, timeout,
  saved format or schema changed.
- **Regression test.** `RocketFillRetirementDemandTest` (8 tests) runs the probe's scenarios for 12,000 ticks after one
  demand. Where the probe saw 61 ticket steps, it asserts:
  - 0 for each of the three holds;
  - 0 for a survivor conflict;
  - exactly three steps, at ticks 100, 300 and 500, for a hold found after the ticket; the entry then ends with its
    tickets released, and a new demand allows three more;
  - that the 200-tick spacing survives a demand made right after the last step;
  - that an entry whose rocket is loaded only by its own tickets stops at three steps and is not revived;
  - the demand queue's bound and order.

  The discovery-bounds case uses the review's ordinary 47 by 32 footprint at an aligned origin. Its checked set is
  5 by 4 = 20 columns, over the limit of 16.

### W130-R4: the cost of the synchronous load, and the move

- **Moved.** A loader's fair-queue visit now only queues its demand (at most 64, one per rocket, RAM only; a repeated
  demand changes nothing). `RocketTransferRecoveryService.tickRetirements`, the coordinator's own tick, begins at most
  four queued demands per tick. Before each `begin` it rechecks the record, the journal reservation, D's projection and
  the escrow rule. The first attempt's synchronous `getChunkAt` on the old site therefore runs in the rocket manager's
  server-tick END handler, not inside the escrow runtime's fair-queue visit.
- **Order unchanged.** Admission refuses the fill (`TRANSFER_JOURNAL_UNPROVED`) before any retirement starts, and a fill
  is admitted only after the checked removal. The first attempt may start one tick later than before; the three
  retire-before-fill GameTests still prove on their second attempt.
- **Worst case.**
  - At most four synchronous old-site loads per tick, and at most 64 per 200 ticks (one per pending rocket).
  - At most three ticket steps per demand. Only the first finds the old site cold; later steps find it held by the
    first ticket.
  - Each level-2 ticket keeps a 5 by 5 area of chunks loaded around each end's origin column: 3 by 3 block-ticking, the
    centre entity-ticking. Only the old site's origin chunk loads synchronously; the rest of the area loads
    asynchronously.
- **Measured.** The native fixture timed the whole ticket step (`ticket_us`) on this machine:
  - first step on a never-generated old site (chunk generation, each case's initial start): 11 samples, 101.5 to
    182.3 ms, mean 135.7 ms;
  - first step on a cold old site saved on disk (HELD_COLUMN's restarts): 2 samples, 2.6 and 3.4 ms;
  - every step that found the old site already loaded: 20 samples, 13 to 50 µs. This includes restarts, where
    recovery's start-up load of both origins had already loaded it.

  Four never-generated sites in one tick could therefore cost about 0.5 to 0.75 s in that tick. A site that was a real
  launch site exists on disk and costs a few milliseconds.
