# W40 record: the space safety return (C18a, ADR-066 section 3.3)

Implementer: Claude (headless). Branch `codex/v1.8.0-w40-safety-return`, based on `38ed9b95`.

## Design (written before the code)

**Scope.** A player adrift in the Space Level is returned to the nearest station safe arrival that
the player may VISIT, or to the Overworld when a complete pass finds none. Nothing new is persisted:
the index and the scheduler are runtime state, rebuilt or cleared with the server.

**Reverse index (`station/model/StationAccessIndex`).** It is held by `StationRegistryModel`, the
synchronized in-memory registry that each `StationRegistrySavedData` owns for the life of one
server. So it is lifecycle-owned, never static.
- It maps an owner or member UUID to a sorted set of station IDs, and keeps a sorted set of every
  committed station ID for operators.
- Bounds: 4,096 stations and at most 33 links each (32 members and the owner), 135,168 links.
  Adding beyond them throws, as the registry itself does.
- It is updated inside the model's own committed mutations: commit (create), delete, add or remove
  a member, accept an invitation, and transfer ownership. Reservations and invitations are not
  access, so they are not indexed. Updating at the model layer keeps it exact for every caller,
  including fixtures that write the registry directly.
- It is rebuilt from the bounded decoded records at load (`restoreStation`), never on a query.
- A generation counter rises on every link change. Lookups are by stable cursor: the next station ID
  after the last one inspected, in UUID order.
- A blocked or quarantined registry answers "unavailable", never an empty candidate set.

**Phase machine (`station/safety/SafetyReturnPhase`, pure).** One per online player.
- A player tick is *adrift* only when all of these hold: the player is an actual connected
  `ServerPlayer` (not a `FakePlayer`, in the player list, not disconnected), alive, not a spectator,
  in the Space Level, not riding and not carrying a passenger, and outside every committed station
  region of an operational registry.
- After 100 consecutive adrift ticks the player is eligible. An attempt (a query job) starts at most
  once per 200 server ticks per player.
- Any non-adrift tick clears the phase and cancels the job. So do a Level change, logout and the
  return itself. Entering a region therefore cancels the job.
- A rocket transfer or an elevator ride is checked when a job is admitted and again at the commit.
  Either one clears the phase. The rocket check fails closed: a blocked journal counts as a transfer.

**Fair resumable query (`station/safety/SafetyReturnQuery`, pure).** Limits: 64 jobs in total and
one per player; 32 candidate inspections per job per tick; 128 inspections and 256 loaded-cell
probes per server tick, globally.
- A job captures the player's position, the index generation and the player's operator status when
  it starts. It keeps a cursor and its best candidate across ticks.
- Each inspection resolves one station: it still exists, the player may VISIT it, and its orbit body
  resolves in the celestial catalog (body and profile).
- Nearest means the smallest squared horizontal distance from the captured position to the arrival
  centre; ties go to the lower station UUID. A candidate is probed only when it would beat the
  current best, so the first safe prefix is never returned.
- Probes are interruptible: the job remembers the candidate and the probe step, and resumes next tick.
- Jobs are served round robin. A job that the budget stopped keeps its place for the next tick.
- When the whole candidate set has been examined:
  - with no best candidate, the result is the Overworld return;
  - otherwise the best is re-inspected and re-probed (the commit). A failure starts a fresh pass.
- A change of the index generation or of the player's operator status starts a fresh pass.
- An unavailable registry or catalog ends the job with a refusal, never with the Overworld.

**Safety probe (`station/safety/SafetyReturnArrival`).** Three probes per arrival: the floor below
the landing pad, then the feet and head cells. It reads only a chunk that `getChunkNow` returns, so
an unloaded arrival is skipped without loading anything. It adds no ticket and no forced chunk.
- The floor must be face-sturdy upwards.
- The feet and head cells must have no collision.
- No cell may hold a fluid or a listed hazard (fire, campfire, magma, berry bush, wither rose, powder
  snow, cobweb, portals).

**Commit and movement (`station/safety/SpaceSafetyReturnService`).**
- The service revalidates that the player is online and still adrift in the same Level, and that no
  transaction has started. A failure here drops the job quietly.
- Protection, as for the elevator ride commit (ADR-059 section 8 step 5):
  - the world border and build height;
  - the public `EndgameEffectEvent` with effect `TELEPORT` and system ID
    `advancedrocketrycommunity:space_safety_return`, with the player as owner and actor.
  - ADR-054 zones are not consulted, because ADR-054 section 6 limits them to endgame effects.
- Coordinates and the move use `SafeCelestialTravel`: the station landing pad, or the Overworld
  shared-spawn surface (its `prepare` for Earth), then its `teleport`.
- A teleport that another mod cancels is a refusal.
- The destination and every refusal are announced through translated chat messages, in English and
  Chinese. Every outcome writes one INFO audit line.

**Wiring.**
- Player ticks use `TickEvent.PlayerTickEvent` (END, server side), and the scheduler uses
  `ServerTickEvent` END.
- The transfer and ride checks are passed in as one predicate from the mod class, so the station
  package does not import the endgame internals.
- A `SpaceSafetyReturnRuntime` bridge exists for GameTests. It is installed at server start and
  cleared at stop.
- No configuration key, no network channel and no schema change.

### Changes from the design

- The commit re-check runs in one tick: one re-inspection and all three re-probes. When fewer than
  three probes remain in the tick's budget, the commit waits for the next tick. A slow commit can
  therefore never mix a stale probe with a fresh one. Candidate probes during a pass stay
  interruptible, as designed.
- Cactus was added to the hazard list.
- An unexpected exception in the query or in one commit is logged as `ARCE_SAFETY_RETURN_FAILED`
  (ERROR) and drops the affected jobs, so the safety return can never stop the server tick. No test
  triggers it, and the GameTest log has none.

## Choices where ADR-066 section 3.3 is open

- **Where the index lives.** It lives in the registry model, which the per-server SavedData owns, so
  it is updated inside the same committed mutation. A listener on `StationManager` would miss writes
  that fixtures and release-test commands make straight to the registry.
- **"Unknown" registry.**
  - A quarantined registry (`updatesAvailable()` false) counts as unknown, as for station BUILD
    protection. The job then ends with a refusal and an announcement, never the Overworld.
  - A blocked registry hides the regions, so nobody counts as adrift and nobody gets a message.
  - A missing celestial catalog is treated like an unknown registry.
- **Body and profile.** A candidate's orbit body must resolve in the current catalog through the
  existing ADR-041 resolver.
- **Who is eligible.**
  - Spectators are not eligible. Creative players are, as in the legacy rule.
  - A player carrying a passenger is treated as in a vehicle.
- **Rocket transfer.** A player is in a transfer when either manifest of a journal record that has not
  committed lists them. A blocked journal holds every player (fail closed).
- **Elevator ride.** A player with a pending ride is held. This is read through
  `EndgameRuntime.devices()`, which is empty while the endgame root is blocked. A blocked root
  cancels every pending ride, so the check then reports none.
- **Protection.** Two of the elevator commit's checks are reused: the world border and build height,
  and the public `EndgameEffectEvent` with effect `TELEPORT`.
  - The event uses the system ID `advancedrocketrycommunity:space_safety_return`, and the player is
    both owner and actor. This posts the endgame API event for a feature that is not an endgame
    device. Section 3.3 asks for the TELEPORT event, and this is the only one there is.
  - Endgame zones are not consulted, because ADR-054 section 6 limits them to endgame effects.
  - Spawn protection is not consulted, because no block changes.
- **An attempt.** An attempt is one admitted query job. A refusal therefore repeats at most every
  200 ticks while the player stays adrift. The message says that another attempt follows in
  10 seconds if the player is still adrift.
- **The Overworld return.** It uses `SafeCelestialTravel.prepare(overworld, EARTH)`: the surface at
  the shared spawn. This is the existing coordinate selection for Earth. It reads the spawn chunk,
  which vanilla keeps loaded. The no-loading rule applies to the station candidates.
- **Player ticks.** The service runs on `TickEvent.PlayerTickEvent` END. The ledger row names the
  legacy `LivingUpdateEvent`. W39 shares that row and may use the living tick instead.

## Not done

- The native "transfer or ride" test does not create a real rocket transfer or elevator ride. A
  synthetic journal record would be picked up by the live rocket recovery service, and a real ride
  needs the whole elevator fixture. The test uses a vehicle and the service's `holdForTest` seam.
  The journal rule itself is unit-tested with real records.
- The 64-job cap, the 32-per-job limit and the 256-probe limit are proved exactly in unit tests. The
  native budget test shows 128 inspections per tick and 32 per job under load. Its candidates are
  unloaded, so it does not reach the probe cap.
- No real-client or dedicated-server visual check was done, and no restart test. The slice persists
  nothing.
- Other C18a rules, W39 and anything in Root's files are out of scope.

## Known risks

- **A protected or cancelled nearest arrival.** If a claim mod cancels the TELEPORT event at the
  nearest authorized station, every attempt refuses again. The player gets one message every
  10 seconds and is never sent to the next station or the Overworld. That follows section 3.3,
  where only a complete pass that finds nothing falls back to the Overworld.
- **Ticked mock players.** Release-test commands that tick mock players (`ReleaseTestPlayers.pump`)
  and leave them in the gap between stations for 100 ticks would now see them returned. The GameTest
  suites are unaffected: their mock players are never ticked, and all 918 tests passed.
- **Using the endgame event.** Mods that listen to `EndgameEffectEvent` and assume an endgame device
  will see a new system ID.
- **Constant station churn.** Under continuous team changes, a job restarts its pass on every
  generation change and may be delayed. Each tick is still bounded.
