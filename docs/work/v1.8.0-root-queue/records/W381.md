## Failure 1: item-cargo replay mismatch

### Tick-level reading of W379's run 1

Source: `D:/GitHub/ARCE-Task-Evidence/root-queue/W379/gt1/latest.log`.

- The batch starts at line 13,631 (07:42:07.438). 3,326 of vanilla's 20-tick progress lines precede it, so it runs in
  server ticks 66,520 to 66,620. The nearest autosaves are ticks 66,000 and 72,000. This is not W375's autosave overlap.
- The fixture paces every tick to 50 ms, so log time converts to ticks.
- Direction 0 (load) is normal: rocket active at 07.562, `OTHER_STATE` WARN at 10.432, which is 57 ticks. The two
  passing runs show the same 57 ticks.
- Direction 1 (unload): rocket active at 10.592. No second WARN follows. The batch's cleanup discards both rockets at
  12.703, about 42 ticks after the rocket appeared. A passing run logs the WARN at 48 ticks.
- So the check failed about six ticks before the replay verifier could have refused anything.

The normal direction-1 timeline, from the traced runs below, counted from the rocket's creation:

| Tick | Event |
|---|---|
| +20 | The device's first paced attempt. |
| +23 | The admission's ledger write is requested (generation G). |
| +25 | G is acknowledged. The scheduler visits the operation, finds the fixture's pause, and waits 20 ticks. |
| +26 | The fixture sees the operation, replaces its frozen digest in the saved ledger, and lifts the pause. |
| +45 | The scheduler visits again; the real replay starts. |
| +48 | The replay verifier refuses the digest: `OTHER_STATE`, WARN. |

### Cause

`EscrowCoordinator.commitPreparations` asks for another ledger generation ten ticks after a write started while an
admission is still unacknowledged. The writer captures that successor's snapshot at the moment G is acknowledged, from
the acknowledged ledger. Then:

1. The fixture replaces the operation in the saved ledger (`ResourceEscrowSavedData.acknowledged`) one tick later. The
   successor is in flight and already holds the admitted operation with its true digest.
2. The successor is acknowledged. Its generation is higher, so `EscrowCoordinator.acknowledge` installs its snapshot. The
   fixture's replacement is gone.
3. The scheduler's visit at G's acknowledgement was deferred by one tick for the busy writer, not by 20 for the pause. So
   the real replay starts at once, verifies the true digest and publishes the first endpoint three ticks later.
4. The fixture's hold check sees a changed endpoint.

This needs an admission write that takes more than ten ticks (0.5 s). A normal one takes two. W379's run shared the
disk with compiling workers. Its failure at +42 fits a write of about 13 ticks: acknowledged near +36, replacement at
+37, successor acknowledged near +38, publication near +41. The old log has no ledger lines, so this attribution of the
W379 failure rests on the timing and on the reproduction, not on a direct observation in that log.

The product behaves correctly here. Only the writer replaces the ledger in production, and a successor captured from
the acknowledged ledger loses nothing. The fixture's direct replacement is the only writer-bypassing step.

## Failure 2: transfer recovery

### Tick-level reading of W380's run

Source: `D:/GitHub/ARCE-Task-Evidence/root-queue/W380/gametest-latest.log`.

- Batch `flight_recovery` starts at line 14,241 (09:51:51.649), in server ticks 70,460 to 70,560. No autosave.
- Source-only case: prepared at 52.518, `saveEverything` until 53.393, proved (one slot) at 53.464.
- Destination-only case: 53.506 to 53.515.
- Both case: transfer `6523d4bd` prepared at 53.542. Nothing is logged for 1.94 s.
- 55.483: the next batch, `monitor_governed`, starts. 55.503: the failure is reported, "both recovery did not
  complete: RETRY_LATER".
- 55.670: `ARCE_TRANSFER_RETIREMENT_PROVED ... slots=2 attempts=1` for `6523d4bd`. 55.720: its `RECOVERED` WARN.

The proof was on its first attempt and finished about 43 ticks after the fixture's first call. The fixture stopped
after 40 calls, three ticks earlier.

**Why the WARN is in another batch.** It belongs to this test's own third transfer. The fixture creates its own
`RocketManager`; the shared barrier's callback completed that manager's return after the test had already failed, and
the WARN was logged then. The two expected WARNs were missing because the test never reached its fourth case, and this
one was counted under `monitor_governed`. No transfer of `monitor_governed` is involved.

**Shared state.** The tests share one thing: the server runtime's `EscrowBarrier`, whose per-Level force window
(`nextForce`) every proof in the run moves. The failed test also left its source rocket `bd91df7c` in the overworld
until a later test cleared the pad at 09:53:29 (line 14,560). It left no journal record.

### Cause

A source-side recovery reports `RECOVERED` only after each slot is forced to disk and read back. From the traced runs:

- A slot's capture is staged for its Level's next force batch (`EscrowBarrier.nextForce`). That batch comes 20 ticks
  after the Level's previous one; when that moment has already passed, it comes on the next barrier tick. A Level's
  **first** batch comes 20 ticks after the capture.
- The force and the read-back run on IO threads in wall-clock time. Their result is delivered on the next barrier tick.
- The both case proves two slots in turn: the source's overworld slot, then the discarded copy's Moon slot.

The fixture allowed 20 calls for one slot and 40 for two, "bounded by two barrier batches". That covers the batch waits
only when no window is fresh and the disk takes no tick:

| Observed | Ticks | Old bound |
|---|---|---|
| Source-only, overworld's first batch (`repro1`, first instance) | 22 | 20: **fails every time** |
| Both, Moon's first batch, 9 ms force (`repro1`, second instance) | 39 | 40: passes with no tick to spare |
| Both, both windows open (`repro1`, later instances) | 18 to 19 | 40 |
| Both in passing full local runs (W378, W379 gt2, W375, W379 gt1) | 18, 25, 26, 28 | 40 |
| Both in W380's failing run | about 43 | 40: fails |

The full-run rows are log time from the both case's `ARCE_TRANSFER_PREPARE` to its `ARCE_TRANSFER_RETIREMENT_PROVED`,
at 50 ms per paced tick.

The product's own allowance for one proof attempt is `ATTEMPT_TICKS = 100`, "a force batch (20 ticks) and its readback,
with margin". The fixture's counts were never the product's bound.

Which of the two effects cost W380 its three ticks cannot be read from its log. With both windows open the batch waits
take 18 ticks, so the passing full runs already spent up to 10 ticks on the two forces. A slower disk under the other
workers' compiles is therefore the likely one. The fix does not depend on the answer.

### The change (`25be40bbe`, `849d2e617`)

- `gametest/RocketTransferRecoveryWait.java` (new) and `gametest/RocketTransferRecoveryGameTests.java`: after each
  `RETRY_LATER` the fixture calls again on the next tick only while all of these hold:
  - the fixture manager still holds the return for that rocket;
  - the return has started at most one attempt;
  - it has recorded no wait or failure reason, other than the barrier's one-tick `BUSY`;
  - at most `ATTEMPT_TICKS` calls were made.

  Otherwise the wait ends at once and the unchanged `RECOVERED` assertion fails with the attempts, ticks, wall time and
  the retirement entry.
- `rocket/server/RocketTransferRecoveryGameTestSupport.java` (new): a read-only view of the manager's retirement entry
  and of `ATTEMPT_TICKS`, for a GameTest server only.
- `RocketTransferRecoveryWaitTest`: a proof in progress outlives the former bounds (fails without the fix); the wait
  ends at the product's allowance, and at once on a second attempt, a dropped return or any reason such as `DEADLINE`,
  `BARRIER_IO`, `SUPERSEDING_SAVE` or `SLOT_UNLOADED`.

What is stricter than before: a recovery that needs a second attempt, or records any wait or fault, now fails on that
tick. Before, it failed only if 40 ticks ran out. What is looser: a clean first attempt may take up to 100 ticks.
The test's `timeoutTicks = 400` is unchanged and still covers the worst case (40 + 100 + 100 plus the short cases).

`RocketTransferRecoveryGameTestSupport` is in `rocket/server`, outside the task's listed `gametest/` and
`resourceescrow/` support paths, because the retirement accessors are package-private there. It follows
`RocketMarginGameTestSupport` and adds no behaviour.

## Known risks

- The new GameTest adds one batch (806 in total), so the HashMap batch order gains one entry. The resize threshold is
  not crossed.
- The new GameTest blocks the ledger IO thread for 15 paced ticks with a 10 s safety timeout. A host that cannot run 15
  ticks in 10 s fails it loudly instead of hanging.
- A legitimate second proof attempt in the recovery fixture (for example a save that supersedes the capture) now fails
  the test on that tick with its reason. Before, such a run also failed, 40 ticks later and without the reason.
