# W186: bounded loaded sealed discovery revisits

## Design note (before implementation)

The write scope is `SealedLoadedDiscovery.java`, its new unit test, and this record. The branch is
`codex/v1.8.0-w186`, based on `073a97f7f45dd41a1a22725cf54ba17db0b0aa7c`. W186 implements Root decision R146;
it does not change an ADR. The initial worktree was clean and other running workers use separate worktrees.

At entry to a loaded chunk, capture its block-entity count N. If a publication or another structural map edit
invalidates the iterator, restart that chunk's iterator rather than advancing to the next chunk. Allow at most N
such restarts during this visit, without replenishing the allowance when the map grows. A stable map still finishes
normally. Removing each published carrier therefore permits the remaining carriers to be discovered in the same
sweep. Repeatedly changing maps eventually exhaust their restart allowance and yield to the next chunk; remaining
carriers remain eligible on the next sweep. The cursor does not copy the map or collect all its positions.
(Superseded under Root decision R155: only the cursor's own publication in the chunk now allows a restart. See
"Review fixes (R155)" below.)

Every iterator advance or restart occupies one caller-charged discovery step. The 256-unit `SealedWorkBudget`, the
64-entry hint queue, publication charges, and the discovery/attempt alternation in `SealedLazyWork.tick` stay
unchanged. Restarts may repeat a non-carrier prefix, so the visit is not guaranteed to be linear in N; each repeated
inspection is still charged and resumes on later ticks. The allowance is local cursor state, not a new saved bound.

Before every entity step, including a restart, the existing Level identity and `getChunkNow` identity checks still
apply. If the Level disappears or the chunk unloads or is replaced, discard that visit immediately, without adding
tickets or loading a chunk. No saved format, journal entry, existing bound, budget, fixture, forced-chunk policy,
status file, ledger, or asset changes. Independent read-only review and integration remain Root's responsibility.

### W186-R1: only the cursor's own publication allows a restart

- `SealedDisassemblyCoordinator` keeps its discovery cursor in a field. When `attemptLazy` gets `PUBLISHED`, it calls
  `SealedLoadedDiscovery.published(dimension, position)`. `clear()` drops the field with the queue.
- `published` grants a restart only when the position lies in the chunk that the cursor is visiting, in the same
  Level.
- The grant applies to the next discovery step only. The materializer edits the map inside `level.setBlock`, before
  `PUBLISHED` returns. Vanilla's `HashMap` iterator then fails at the very next `next()` call. If that step does not
  fail, the publication replaced the block entity in place, which is not a structural edit, and the grant ends unused.
- A `ConcurrentModificationException` restarts the iterator only with a grant, and still at most N times per visit,
  where N is the chunk's block-entity count at entry. Any other edit, such as a piston or a placed chest, ends the
  visit, as before W186.
- The review left the shape of the grant open. It suggested a counter that adds one restart per publication. I chose
  the one-step grant because it is stricter: an in-place publication cannot leave a spare restart for a later foreign
  edit. Both shapes allow no more restarts than there are publications in the chunk, and no more than N.
- A publication that does not come from the lazy attempt, such as the direct `RocketManager` call to `unseal`,
  grants nothing. If it edits the visited chunk, the cursor moves on, as before W186.
- The budget, the alternation in `SealedLazyWork.tick`, the 64-entry queue, the journal, the saved formats and every
  bound are unchanged. The grant and the allowance are RAM fields of the cursor.

### W186-R2: time cost of a visit

Let N be the chunk's block-entity count at entry, M the largest map size during the visit, T the number of ticks
between foreign structural edits, and P the number of carriers that lazy attempts publish in the chunk during the
visit. With an empty hint queue, discovery gets all 256 units of a tick. While hints are queued, attempts take part
of each tick.

- **No publication of its own (P = 0).** This includes a busy chunk without carriers, and one whose carriers are held.
  The cursor leaves at the first structural edit after entry, or after one complete scan. With an empty queue this is
  at most min(T + 1, ⌈(N + 3) / 256⌉) ticks, which is the cost before W186. The edit term does not depend on the
  budget. The scan term grows with the share that attempts take.
- **With publications (P > 0).** There are at most min(P, N) restarts, and each rescans from the first entry. The work
  is therefore at most (P + 1) × (M + 3) advances. It is quadratic when the carriers lie behind many ordinary block
  entities: 5,000 ordinary block entities and 100 carriers take 492 ticks, about 25 seconds, while every other chunk
  waits. This cost arises only while carriers are actually being published.
- **A busy chunk with carriers.** A foreign edit that falls between a publication and the next discovery step is
  absorbed by that publication's restart. Any other foreign edit ends the visit. The carriers left in that chunk then
  wait for the next sweep, as before W186.

  chunk of the visited Level restarts the iterator, and only at the next step.

### Not done and known risks

- No complete GameTest, `runData`, full `build`, native case or visual check ran in this session. No status file,
  ledger, ADR or Gate was changed.
- In a chunk whose map changes often, and which also holds members beyond the hint bound, those members publish only
  a few per sweep, as before W186. This is the trade that R155 chose. The W184 tail can return in such a chunk.
- The grant depends on vanilla's fail-fast `HashMap` iterator failing at the step right after the publication. If a
  mod replaced a chunk's block-entity map with one that detects changes later, a publication would not restart the
  iterator, and the remaining carriers would wait for the next sweep. The cursor still cannot be held, because every
  restart needs a grant.
- The cost of a visit with publications stays quadratic in the worst case (492 ticks for 100 carriers behind 5,000
  ordinary block entities). Every step is still charged to the 256-unit tick allowance.
